    OPS NOTE · Distance From Earth

    RIDGE HAZARD D

    On a Mars transit there is no evacuation option and one-way light time reaches ~22 minutes. AstroPulse is therefore offline-first by design: risk is scored on board, the astronaut gets a directed action immediately, and only the trend package waits for the downlink window. Distance from Earth is treated as a design constraint, not an error state.

    03

    System Architecture — The Backend

    EDGE-FIRST · STORE-AND-FORWARD

    There is no cloud round-trip in the loop — on a Mars transit, Earth can be 20 minutes away one-way, so the backend is offline-first by design. An onboard service on the crew tablet ingests, scores and stores everything locally in an append-only event log. A ground service in Houston only receives compressed trend packages during downlink windows, through an outbox queue with retry. This is the same local-first pipeline this prototype actually runs in your browser: cut the link above and watch packages queue, then restore it and watch them sync. Below is exactly what each stage does with a second of your heartbeat.

    STAGE 01

    INGEST

    BLE 5.0 · chest strap / patch

    Raw ECG at 250 Hz and PPG from the crew wearable stream into the onboard service. Packets are timestamped to mission clock and sequence-checked.

    IN ecg_raw, ppg_raw
    OUT 1 Hz telemetry frame
    >
    STAGE 02

    SIGNAL PROCESSING

    On-device DSP · Pan-Tompkins

    R-peak detection extracts heart rate and RR intervals; RMSSD yields HRV. Motion artifacts and dropouts are rejected before any metric is trusted.

    IN telemetry frame
    OUT hr, hrv, spo2, temp
    >
    STAGE 03

    RISK ENGINE

    Deterministic rules · HRP ranges

    Physiology is fused with the daily check-in and scored against NASA HRP reference ranges. Output is a 0–100 Wellness Risk Index plus the flight rule that fired — always explainable.

    IN metrics + check-in
    OUT risk index, actions
    >
    STAGE 04

    LOCAL STORE

    SQLite · encrypted at rest

    Full-resolution data never leaves the vehicle by default. 90 days of trends are kept on board; the crew member's record is theirs, access-logged for the medical officer.

    RETENTION 90 days onboard
    ACCESS subject + CMO
    >
    STAGE 05

    DOWNLINK SYNC

    Store-and-forward · TDRS / DSN

    During each comm window, a compressed trend package — indices, flags, check-ins, no raw ECG — syncs to the ground service. Built for 22-minute Mars light delay.

    PKG ~40 KB / sol
    CADENCE per comm window

    04

    Why — The Rules Behind Every Verdict

    NO BLACK BOX · PUBLISHED MATHS

    Every status on this console comes from one of five published rules, scored against your own baseline — the same pattern as SpaceGenes+, the 2025 Global Winner for Best Use of Science, whose whole product was one dataset and one checkable rule (flag a gene when FCcombined > 1.5 × (FCradiation + FCmicrogravity)). Ours is the physiological version: the combination of heart + mood is the insight.

    RuleTrigger — vs your own baselineStatusAction shown
    R1Resting HR above your baseline mean + 2 SD, sustainedAMBERRest, slow breathing, re-check in 15 min
    R2R1 and mood ≤ 2/5 today — heart and mood deteriorating togetherREDLog symptoms, reduce workload, notify flight surgeon (queued until the link returns)
    R3Mood ≤ 2/5 for 2 days with heart rate normalAMBERSleep / light / activity countermeasure, journal prompt, re-check tomorrow
    R4HR back within ±1 SD of baseline and mood ≥ 3/5GREEN“Back to your normal — continue mission”
    R5No fresh heart data (stream held / sensor stale) or no check-in todaySTALE“Check your wearable, log mood manually” — AstroPulse never shows green on missing data. Try the Hold button on TLM-01.
    05

    NASA Evidence — And What Past Winners Did

    ONE DATASET · ONE RULE · ONE DECISION

    We studied the 2023–2025 Global Winners before building this. No vital-signs monitoring app won globally in those years — SpaceGenes+ (2025) is the closest — and the winners share one shape: one named NASA dataset, used live, feeding one simple decision for one named user. AstroPulse is built to that shape, anchored to the one dataset that measured exactly what we monitor: heart rate and mood in flight.

    ANCHOR DATASET · HR + MOOD
    Inspiration4 — HR/HRV really do change in flight

    On the first all-civilian orbital mission (4 crew, 3 days, Apple Watch Series 6), heart rate and HRV changed significantly across mission phases (HR F=37.10, p<0.0001; HRV F=5.64, p=0.0046), and mood/alertness was surveyed on an 11-point scale (Jones et al., Nature 2024; data via OSDR / SOMA). Our synthetic stream is shaped to that kind of variation — it is not Inspiration4 raw data, and a 3-day, n=4 mission does not prove our thresholds.

    PubMed 38862026
    EVIDENCE BASE · OSDR
    OSD-288 — why radiation + microgravity compound

    In GeneLab study OSD-288 (“Transcriptome analysis of murine spleen in space”), combined radiation + microgravity synergistically up-regulated DNA-damage genes (TP53, ATM, GADD45A) — the dataset SpaceGenes+ built its winning dashboard on. AstroPulse tracks the physiological echo of that combined stress: HR/HRV plus mood, together. OSD-288 is a mouse transcriptome study — it contains no human heart data, and we claim no causal link.

    NASA OSDR
    WINNER PATTERNS · 2023–2025
    What actually won — copied deliberately

    SpaceGenes+ (2025, Best Use of Science): one dataset, one published rule, three-step UX, no backend at all. SkySense (2025, Best Use of Technology, Mexico): one plain-language question — “Will it rain on my parade?” — answered with a decision, not a chart. Landsat Connect (2024, Best Mission Concept): a “simple, intuitive” app whose core feature is a timely notification. AstroPulse: one question (“Am I OK — for me?”), one rule set, one action per alert.

    SpaceGenes+ live site
    What AstroPulse does not do — stated plainly

    Prototype for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026. The live heart/mood stream is synthetic demo data. AstroPulse does not diagnose, is not a medical device, is not clinically validated, does not replace the flight surgeon, and is not endorsed by NASA. Rules R1–R5 are prototype rules against a personal baseline — no NASA-published clinical HR/mood alert threshold for astronauts exists, which is exactly why we compare each astronaut to themselves. Built for astronauts — the same loop serves anyone far from a doctor: remote clinics, ships, polar stations, night-shift crews.

    06

    Design Basis — NASA RIDGE Hazards

    REF: NASA HUMAN RESEARCH PROGRAM

    Every AstroPulse signal maps to a documented spaceflight hazard. The system is scoped to what it can honestly measure — heart and mood, continuously.

    RIDGE · R
    Radiation

    Exposure log is carried alongside vitals, so symptoms are never interpreted out of context.

    RIDGE · I
    Isolation & Confinement

    Daily mood, energy and stress trends — the primary focus of this prototype.

    RIDGE · D
    Distance from Earth

    Offline-first scoring and onboard directed actions; no ground round-trip required.

    RIDGE · G
    Gravity Fields

    Resting HR and HRV tracked against microgravity deconditioning, ~1% bone-density loss per month.

    RIDGE · E
    Hostile / Closed Env.

    SpO2 and core temperature monitored continuously inside a closed-loop habitat.
